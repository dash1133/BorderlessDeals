# Borderless Deals: message house (home page, v2 draft)

## Core message (never changes)

**Sell to the world.**
**AI-enabled platform for cross-border M&A.**

Support line, used once in the hero and echoed in the final CTA:
> Borderless Deals puts US companies in front of qualified buyers abroad, starting with India, and carries each deal from anonymous teaser to closing. Brokers bring the companies. AI agents do the work. People make every decision.

## Discipline rules

1. **One reader on the home page:** the sell side, meaning US owners and the brokers who represent them. Buyers, PE and providers get one link each to their own pages, not their own home sections.
2. **One claim per section.** Every section answers the next question a seller would ask about selling to the world, in this order: *Why abroad? → Why doesn't it happen today? → How does Borderless do it? → Where does the AI come in? → How do buyers find me? → Will the deal hold? → Who does the cross-border work? → Is it confidential? → What does it cost my broker? → Where can I sell today? → Objections → Act.*
3. **Every headline ladders up to "Sell to the world."** If a headline would make sense on any SaaS site, rewrite it.
4. **Every number comes from the memo, with its source named.** No invented figures in copy.
5. **Fixed vocabulary** (use these words and no synonyms):

| Use | Don't use |
|---|---|
| AI agent (first mention per section), then agent | bot, assistant, copilot |
| broker (sell-side / buy-side when it matters) | adviser, intermediary, banker |
| buyer | acquirer, investor (except PE) |
| anonymous teaser | listing, ticker, profile |
| deal room | data room (except for the disclosure tier), workspace |
| corridor | market, route, lane |
| platform | marketplace (except "services marketplace"), network |
| closing / close | exit (except PE) |

6. **Banned claims:** guaranteed outcomes; "the platform is a broker"; any success fee or % of the deal; "global" without "starting with US–India"; testimonials or logos.

## Sections

### S1 · Hero
- **Job:** in five seconds, show what this is, who it's for and the first action.
- **Eyebrow:** Now live · US ↔ India corridor
- **H1:** Sell to the world.
- **Category line:** AI-enabled platform for cross-border M&A.
- **Support:** Borderless Deals puts US companies in front of qualified buyers abroad, starting with India, and carries each deal from anonymous teaser to closing. Brokers bring the companies. AI agents do the work. People make every decision.
- **CTAs:** [List a company] (for brokers) · [Find companies to buy] → buyers page
- **Assurance line:** Free for founding brokers · You keep your client and your fee · No percentage of the deal
- **Visual, hero-corridor:** a US company's anonymous teaser travels along an arc to a buyer in India; the two AI agents confirm fit; an NDA is signed. In-image caption: "Sell to the world."

### S2 · The opportunity: why sell abroad
- **Claim:** the buyers for US companies are increasingly abroad.
- **Eyebrow:** The opportunity
- **H2:** Your next buyer may not be in the US.
- **Body:** A great deal of foreign capital is waiting to be invested in US companies. Foreign buyers can value market entry, technology or capacity differently, and more bidders means more competition for a company.
- **Proof:**
  - Japanese buyers deployed more than $100B into North America in 2025 (Morrison Foerster).
  - India's outbound M&A reached $13.7B in 2025, up from $5.1B in 2024 (EY India).
  - About 1 million viable US small businesses will need buyers by 2035, up to about $5T in enterprise value (McKinsey).
- **Visual, why-now:** counters showing foreign demand ($100B+, $13.7B) meeting US supply (~1M, ~$5T).

### S3 · The problem: why it doesn't happen today
- **Claim:** mid-market companies rarely reach foreign buyers, because of complexity, not lack of capital.
- **Eyebrow:** The problem
- **H2:** Most companies only ever meet domestic buyers.
- **Body:** Cross-border deals are hard to find and hard to finish. Large companies have banks with global desks; mid-market owners and their local brokers do not. The binding constraint is information and execution complexity, not capital.
- **Proof:**
  - Cross-border deals fell from about 50% of global deal value in 2007 to about 30% today (BCG).
  - Deals above $1B make up about 76% of cross-border value (Oliver Wyman).
  - 70% of advisory firms complete three or fewer transactions a year (Axial).
- **Seven barriers:** Discovery · Information · Language · Regulation · Trust · Execution cost · Fragmented brokers.
- **Visual, barriers:** seven walls between a US seller and a buyer in India; an AI agent turns each into its fix.

### S4 · The platform: how Borderless does it
- **Claim:** one platform takes a company from listing to closing, across borders.
- **Eyebrow:** How it works
- **H2:** One platform, from anonymous teaser to closing.
- **Three pillars:**
  1. **Directory** ("LinkedIn for companies"): every buyer, seller, broker, PE fund and service provider is registered.
  2. **Discovery:** buyers find companies, sellers find buyers, brokers find both, across borders and languages.
  3. **Services and execution** ("Upwork for deal services"): hire legal, tax, valuation, financing and accounting providers, then run LOI, diligence, SPA and closing in one deal room.
- **Visual, pillars:** the app cycling through the Directory, Discovery and Services tabs.

### S5 · The AI: agents do the work, people decide
- **Claim:** every participant is represented by an AI agent, not a static profile.
- **Eyebrow:** AI-enabled
- **H2:** An AI agent represents your company to buyers everywhere.
- **Body:** Each participant has an AI agent. It writes and maintains the company's page, answers other agents' questions within the disclosure rules you set, in the reader's language, and searches the market around the clock. Price, terms and anything binding always go to a person.
- **Points:** writes and maintains the company page · answers buyers' agents within your disclosure rules · works in every reader's language · escalates anything binding to a person.
- **Visual, agent-wiki:** a company page writes itself, switches language, and answers a buyer agent's question with a cited answer "within disclosure rules".

### S6 · Discovery: how buyers find you
- **Claim:** buyers find your anonymous teaser where they already search; there is no cold outreach.
- **Eyebrow:** Reach
- **H2:** Buyers find your teaser wherever they search.
- **Body:** Anonymous teasers are discoverable on the platform, inside Claude and ChatGPT, and on Google. Buyers browse free; to sign an NDA or contact the broker, they join. When a buyer's AI agent finds a fit, it qualifies with your agent first, and names are revealed only when both sides opt in.
- **Visual, ai-assistant:** a buyer asks an AI assistant for US companies for sale; anonymous teasers appear; "Sign NDA → join Borderless".

### S7 · Deals that hold: stage gates
- **Claim:** problems surface at the mandate, not after months of exclusivity.
- **Eyebrow:** Deals that hold
- **H2:** Find the deal-breakers before the buyer does.
- **Body:** Most broken deals fail on things that were knowable earlier. At every stage an AI agent tests the seller's and the buyer's stories for contradictions and missing information, from the mandate to the SPA. An adviser is called in for anything deeper, and the broker decides.
- **Proof:** In Axial's review of 65 broken LOIs, non-QoE diligence findings caused 21.5% of failures and QoE EBITDA discrepancies 15.4%. Deals sat in exclusivity for 58 to 203 days before they broke.
- **Gates:** Mandate → Collateral → Anonymous teaser → NDA → LOI → Due diligence → SPA and closing.
- **Visual, stage-gates:** a deal moves through the gates; a contradiction is flagged at the mandate, fixed with an adviser, and the deal proceeds to closing.

### S8 · Cross-border execution: services
- **Claim:** the specialists a cross-border deal needs are hired in the same flow.
- **Eyebrow:** Closing across borders
- **H2:** The cross-border specialists you need, hired in the same flow.
- **Body:** Float a requirement, and vetted providers bid with scope, price and timeline, or you invite them directly. The AI agent compares proposals, a person decides, and the work lands in the deal room. Categories at launch: financing, legal, accounting, tax and valuation.
- **Visual, services-marketplace:** a requirement is floated, providers bid, the best fit is hired, and a deliverable lands in the deal room.

### S9 · Confidentiality
- **Claim:** you control who sees what, at every step.
- **Eyebrow:** Confidential by design
- **H2:** Sell to the world without telling the world.
- **Body:** Information is released in tiers: anonymous teaser, then named profile under NDA, then the deal room. Each release is approved by the seller or broker, and every disclosure is logged. Buyers pass KYC and AML checks before names are revealed. Borderless Deals never holds escrow or payments; licensed providers do.
- **Visual, trust-tiers:** three nested tiers unlock as a buyer passes KYC and signs the NDA, while an access log records each step.

### S10 · Built around brokers: the offer
- **Claim:** brokers bring the companies, and the platform gives them global reach at no cost to join.
- **Eyebrow:** For brokers
- **H2:** Brokers bring the companies. We give them global reach.
- **Body:** Borderless Deals is not a broker and never competes with yours. At launch, companies are listed only through brokers.
- **Founding Broker Offer:** a free seat in the US–India corridor · an AI agent that builds the company page, anonymous teaser and buyer list on day one · teasers published inside Claude, ChatGPT and Google · stage-gate checks from the mandate · vetted cross-border providers · ratings and closed-deal record · one system for domestic and cross-border mandates.
- **Promise:** You keep your mandate, your client and your fee. The platform never takes a percentage of your deal.
- **Visual, offer-stack:** offer items stack with ticks and end on "Founding broker: $0 to join".

### S11 · Corridors
- **Claim:** Borderless starts with one corridor, done well.
- **Eyebrow:** Where you can sell today

- **Body:** We launch corridor by corridor, where supply and demand are both strong. The first pairs the largest pool of sellers and brokers (the US) with a fast-growing base of outbound buyers (India). Japan and the transatlantic corridor are candidates for next.
- **Visual, corridors-roadmap:** the US–India arc is live; dashed arcs to Japan and Europe are marked "next".
